Small teams need a repeatable ship gate, not a heroic deploy.

Production incidents often come from missing preflight checks, unclear rollback ownership, and deploying without a minimal smoke-test plan. A lightweight ship gate can reduce avoidable risk without creating enterprise process overhead.

Before deployment

  • Confirm the exact commit or artifact being released.
  • Identify affected services, migrations, secrets, and external dependencies.
  • Define the rollback or compensation path before the change starts.
  • Verify monitoring, logs, and the owner who will watch the release.
  • Write the smallest meaningful smoke test for the customer path.

After deployment

Run the smoke test, inspect errors and key business events, record the release outcome, and keep the rollback window open until the evidence is stable. Avoid treating “deployment succeeded” as proof that the customer workflow succeeded.

Hotfix discipline

A hotfix should identify the production symptom, bounded change, verification step, and rollback path. Avoid combining unrelated cleanup or upgrades with an urgent production repair.
